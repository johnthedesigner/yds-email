Hello dahlia friends!

The temperatures are rising and the days are getting longer. Packages will soon be arriving on our doorsteps, while at home our overwintered tubers begin to sprout. We can’t wait for the beautiful weather to come.

No matter where in the world you go, flower farms are increasingly popular as more and more dahlia growers take their passion to the next level. Flower CSA’s and bouquet subscriptions abound and small flower farms are thriving everywhere. We looked to Australia for our April Zoom meeting, and we were so fortunate to find Jen Foster and Erica Downes, two flower farmers who have combined their talent for growing flowers with floral design to bring a new level of success to their individual businesses. Erica’s Floral Design workshops and Jen’s Farm Experiences are fabulous and will inspire you to dream big for your own garden. Be sure to save the date. You won’t want to miss their presentation!

Our online tuber store is still open, and we have been adding more stock along with new cultivars over the past month. Be sure to check it out and drop a few new cultivars in your cart before they’re gone. YDS will hold our third annual public sale April 20 & 21 at Mass Hort. We’ll have tubers and rooted dahlia plants at our April sale and there’s sure to be something new there you will want to add to your garden. See more information below and to sign up to volunteer to help with the sale.

Our March meeting at Spence Farm was fabulous! So many of you came to learn all about irrigation from YDS member and watering system wizard John Livornese. John gave an excellent presentation about the various methods of setting up an efficient and reliable watering system. There were lots of questions during his presentation and more conversations afterward. Our thanks to John for sharing with us what he's learned about watering systems through his own gardening journey.

– Misty & Carol

Member Spotlight

Cheryl Piacentini

Photos by Cheryl Piacentini

I have always had a passion for arranging flowers. Three years ago I purchased a dahlia CSA from Emily of Grounded Flower Farm and fell in love. The timing was perfect! As a new empty nester, I was in need of something to nurture. I decided to give growing dahlias a try. 2022 was my first growing season. I had never grown anything in the past and was a complete novice.

I bought Erin Benzakein’s (Floret Flowers) book “Discovering Dahlias” and Connie Thompson’s book “Connie's Dahlias” (recommend both). I also started to follow several cut flower farmers on social media. My husband put together 5 raised beds for me and the adventure began. I had a lot of tuber loss to rot in the spring. I think my soil in the raised beds was too dense. Good thing I overbought on tubers! Despite the rocky start I did get a fair amount of lovely dahlia blooms.

My absolute favorite the first year of growing was Coralie. She bloomed early and consistently for me all summer. Other favorites where Camano Zoe, Take Off, and Kenora Lisa. There were many ups and downs throughout the first season. Rotted tubers, bugs, plants that took forever to grow then got hit by frost before ever blooming. I have absolutely loved learning all that is involved in dahlia growing. I am fascinated with the science behind plant behavior. My focus for dahlias this year is to start earlier and improve on soil health.

Last year I expanded my dahlia plants to approximately 120-130 with about 80 varieties. As much as I love dahlias, I was frustrated in my first season with their slow growth, not having many blooms until late August. I have added spring bulbs to my growing plan. I have 200 tulips and about 75 ranunculus plants currently growing in my beds. Additionally I am experimenting in growing from seed several varieties including: zinnia, stock, snapdragon, straw flower, cress and statice. My dream is to be able to go out into my yard and create lovely mixed bouquets using only products grown and arranged by myself. Growing and arranging flowers brings me joy. I hope this year to have many, many blooms to share with my family, friends, and community and spread the happiness and joy that flowers bring!

Cheryl gardens in Topsfield MA

American Dahlia Society 2024 New Introductions

Part 2 (continued from last month) A Look at Some of the ADS Medals Awarded Annually

by Carol Palmer & Misty Florez

Last month we were introduced to undisseminated seedlings, which are hybridized cultivars not available to the general public. Any hybridizer can submit their undisseminated seedlings to be formally evaluated and scored through the ADS system of Trial Gardens or at ADS sanctioned local dahlia shows (Seedling Bench Evaluation) with the goal of being officially recognized by ADS and possibly even winning a medal.

ADS recognizes cultivar excellence through awarding medals. There are currently five ADS medals awarded annually:

  • The Derrill W. Hart Medal
  • The Lynn B. Dudley Medal
  • The Evie Gullikson Medal
  • The Stanley Johnson Medal
  • The Les Connell Medal

In order to be considered for official recognition by ADS, a cultivar must score 85 points or higher out of 100 points. Let’s take a look at three of those medals that are awarded only to undisseminated seedlings that have scored the highest in trial gardens or on a local show seedling bench:

  • The Derrill W. Hart Medal: awarded to the cultivar that has the highest average Trial Garden score in the ADS accredited trial gardens during the previous year as an:
    • AA or A; B ; BB; M; Ball, Miniature Ball, or Pompon; ST or NX; and Waterlily
    • The highest 3 scores above 85 are used to determine the highest average.
  • Lynn B. Dudley Medal; awarded to the cultivar that has the highest average Seedling Bench Evaluation score as an:
    • AA or A; B ; BB; M; Ball, Miniature Ball, or Pompon; ST or NX; and Waterlily
    • The highest 3 scores above 85 are used to determine the highest average.
  • Evie Gullikson Medal: awarded to the cultivar that has the highest average Seedling Bench Evaluation score above 85 and to the cultivar with the highest average Trial Garden score above 85 as an open center or disc center

In case of ties, duplicate medals will be awarded. Take a look at some of the new introductions for 2024 that have been awarded one of these three medals:
